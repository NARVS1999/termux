# Product Manager Roadmap: Fundamentals → Intermediate

> General knowledge for product managers, with product-management-specific context.

## Product Fundamentals (Prerequisites)

- [ ] **Phase 1** — What is PM: role, scope, responsibilities, success metrics
- [ ] **Phase 2** — Product lifecycle: ideation, validation, build, launch, iterate
- [ ] **Phase 3** — Stakeholders: users, engineers, design, sales, leadership
- [ ] **Phase 4** — Product types: SaaS, consumer, platform, internal tools

## Discovery & Research

- [ ] **Phase 5** — User research: interviews, observation, pain points
- [ ] **Phase 6** — Market research: TAM/SAM/SOM, trends, market needs
- [ ] **Phase 7** — Competitive analysis: positioning, features, moats
- [ ] **Phase 8** — Problem validation: problem statements, assumptions, evidence
- [ ] **Phase 9** — Opportunity identification: customer jobs, gaps, signals
- [ ] **Phase 10** — UX basics: user flows, prototypes, usability testing

## Strategy & Vision

- [ ] **Phase 11** — Product vision: long-term direction, narrative
- [ ] **Phase 12** — Strategy frameworks: product-market fit, strategy lenses
- [ ] **Phase 13** — Goals & OKRs: objectives, key results, alignment
- [ ] **Phase 14** — Roadmapping: themes, horizons, tradeoffs
- [ ] **Phase 15** — Positioning: value proposition, differentiation, messaging

## Prioritization & Planning

- [ ] **Phase 16** — Prioritization: RICE, MoSCoW, effort-impact, Kano
- [ ] **Phase 17** — MVP definition: scope slicing, learning goals
- [ ] **Phase 18** — Release planning: milestones, dependencies, sequencing
- [ ] **Phase 19** — Agile & scrum: ceremonies, roles, estimation
- [ ] **Phase 20** — Backlog management: grooming, refinement, hygiene

## Execution & Delivery

- [ ] **Phase 21** — Sprint cycles: planning, standups, reviews, retros
- [ ] **Phase 22** — Stakeholder communication: updates, decisions, escalations
- [ ] **Phase 23** — Acceptance criteria: defining done, quality bars
- [ ] **Phase 24** — Shipping: launch plans, coordination, rollouts
- [ ] **Phase 25** — Risk management: dependencies, blockers, mitigation

## Metrics & Analytics

- [ ] **Phase 26** — Metric frameworks: AARRR, HEART, north star
- [ ] **Phase 27** — Instrumentation: events, properties, data quality
- [ ] **Phase 28** — Funnel analysis: conversion, drop-off, leakage
- [ ] **Phase 29** — Experimentation: hypotheses, A/B tests, statistical basics
- [ ] **Phase 30** — Dashboarding: reporting, insights, stakeholder views

## Pricing & Monetization

- [ ] **Phase 31** — Pricing models: subscription, usage-based, freemium
- [ ] **Phase 32** — Packaging: tiers, bundles, plan boundaries
- [ ] **Phase 33** — Monetization: revenue levers, churn, expansion
- [ ] **Phase 34** — Business models: unit economics, LTV/CAC

## AI & Technical Product Skills

- [ ] **Phase 35** — AI product fundamentals: models, use cases, limits
- [ ] **Phase 36** — LLM capabilities: prompting, RAG, agents, evaluation
- [ ] **Phase 37** — Data literacy: metrics, SQL basics, data interpretation
- [ ] **Phase 38** — Technical acumen: architecture basics, feasibility, tradeoffs
- [ ] **Phase 39** — API & integration thinking: platforms, ecosystem

## Communication & Influence

- [ ] **Phase 40** — Storytelling: narratives, customer stories, data
- [ ] **Phase 41** — Presentations: structure, clarity, executive updates
- [ ] **Phase 42** — Documentation: PRDs, specs, decision memos
- [ ] **Phase 43** — Negotiation: alignment, conflict resolution, influence

## Career & Growth

- [ ] **Phase 44** — PM competencies: discovery, execution, leadership
- [ ] **Phase 45** — Interviews: product sense, metrics, behavioral rounds
- [ ] **Phase 46** — Mentorship: feedback, coaching, growth plans
- [ ] **Phase 47** — Continuous learning: books, case studies, communities

## Recommended Learning Path (priority order)

1. **Phase 1–4** — Product fundamentals
2. **Phase 5–10** — Discovery & research
3. **Phase 11–15** — Strategy & vision
4. **Phase 16–20** — Prioritization & planning
5. **Phase 26–30** — Metrics & analytics
6. **Phase 40–43** — Communication & influence
7. **Phase 35–39** — AI & technical skills

> Focus on building real products — employers hire mid-level product managers for clean, user-focused, well-measured releases plus ownership of products end-to-end.
